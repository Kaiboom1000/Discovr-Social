# 17 - Revenue Model and Unit Economics

Revenue Model and Unit Economics defines a core part of the Discovr product specification. This chapter is written as a professional operating document for product, design, engineering, operations, and business planning.

Discovr is a social discovery platform with familiar social behavior and a differentiated distribution model. The platform should help people, content, projects, and communities reach relevant audiences without making existing popularity the only path to evaluation.

The central product promise remains consistent across the specification: eligible content should receive a meaningful first opportunity with a relevant audience, but expansion must depend on measured response, safety, quality, and user value.

This chapter should be treated as a working source of truth. It is intentionally neutral, implementation oriented, and written to reduce ambiguity across product, design, engineering, business, and operations decisions.

## Chapter position

This chapter connects product strategy to economic sustainability. It defines how revenue, costs, market entry, and operations should be evaluated.

The focus areas for this chapter are revenue streams, cost structure, gross margin, storage costs, delivery costs, scenario planning. These areas should be defined with enough depth that future contributors can build, evaluate, and operate the product without guessing the intent.

## Purpose

Revenue Model and Unit Economics is part of the operating system of Discovr, not a detached feature list. The chapter should define how revenue streams, cost structure, gross margin, storage costs, delivery costs support the platform promise that eligible work receives a meaningful first opportunity and that users retain control over relevance. This chapter connects product strategy to economic sustainability. It defines how revenue, costs, market entry, and operations should be evaluated.

The product decision in this area should be evaluated by whether it improves meaningful discovery, protects feed quality, preserves trust, and creates maintainable implementation paths. A feature that increases short term activity while weakening the platform promise should be redesigned before it becomes a dependency.

Advertising is the second monetization priority and should use privacy preserving audience segments, contextual signals, and interest based metadata rather than selling personal data. This principle should be applied directly to Revenue Model and Unit Economics. The product should not create hidden incentives that push teams toward unclear distribution, unclear billing, unclear ranking, or unclear user control.

The first version should be practical, but the architecture should not trap the product in a weak foundation. Discovr should avoid overbuilding early workflows, but it should still define the states, permissions, metrics, and review paths that will matter once usage grows.

Implementation should separate user facing simplicity from internal precision. The interface can remain calm and minimal while the system records eligibility, visibility, ownership, state changes, event history, trust signals, and operational decisions in a structured way.

The chapter should be read as a decision standard. When later contributors need to choose between speed, growth, monetization, safety, and trust, the correct answer is the option that strengthens Discovr as a discovery first social platform rather than only a high activity feed.

## Commercial model detail

The preferred monetization order is subscriptions and Preserve Packs first, advertising second, creator advertising and sponsorship tools third, and privacy preserving metadata based targeting as the supporting targeting model. This order matters because the product should first prove that users will pay for durable utility before leaning heavily on paid distribution.

Subscriptions should unlock capability rather than guarantee reach. Higher limits, better analytics, advanced project tools, scheduling, team seats, storage, profile customization, business workflows, and collaboration tools are appropriate paid features. Organic first tests should remain available to eligible content regardless of payment status.

Preserve Packs should be designed as storage and permanence products. They can allow users or creators to keep additional work available, increase archival capacity, preserve project history, or extend media retention. The product should avoid implying that Preserve Packs purchase ranking preference.

Advertising should be clearly labeled, reviewed, paced, capped, and measured separately from organic distribution. Sponsored posts, sponsored projects, search placements, and community sponsorships can become strong revenue streams only if users can distinguish paid placements from platform recommendations.

Creator sponsorship tools should help creators and brands find each other through transparent briefs, eligibility rules, pricing support, disclosure requirements, campaign tracking, and brand safety controls. The marketplace should not pressure creators into undisclosed promotion.

Creator payouts should use a disciplined ledger. The platform needs wallet balances, pending balances, available balances, refund reserves, payout thresholds, fraud checks, identity verification where required, statements, payment provider records, tax information where applicable, and support paths for disputes.

Revenue modeling should connect every plan and ad product to cost. Storage, media delivery, processing, moderation, search indexing, support, payment fees, fraud reserves, chargebacks, infrastructure, and internal tooling all affect margin. Growth without cost visibility can make the product look healthier than it is.

The advertising data model should avoid selling personal data. Targeting should rely on broad audience segments, declared interests, contextual placement, community or topic relevance, aggregated metadata, and privacy preserving measurement. Users should have controls that explain ad personalization in plain language.

Billing design should preserve trust. Users need clear upgrade pages, plan comparison, proration rules, renewal dates, cancellation paths, invoices, receipts, failed payment handling, grace periods, downgrade behavior, and entitlement changes that do not surprise them.

Creator monetization should not be activated without fraud controls. The platform must guard against fake engagement, payout farming, stolen accounts, duplicate content, chargeback abuse, advertiser fraud, misleading sponsorships, and attempts to convert organic distribution into hidden paid distribution.

Billing design should preserve trust. Users need clear upgrade pages, plan comparison, proration rules, renewal dates, cancellation paths, invoices, receipts, failed payment handling, grace periods, downgrade behavior, and entitlement changes that do not surprise them.

Creator monetization should not be activated without fraud controls. The platform must guard against fake engagement, payout farming, stolen accounts, duplicate content, chargeback abuse, advertiser fraud, misleading sponsorships, and attempts to convert organic distribution into hidden paid distribution.

The preferred monetization order is subscriptions and Preserve Packs first, advertising second, creator advertising and sponsorship tools third, and privacy preserving metadata based targeting as the supporting targeting model. This order matters because the product should first prove that users will pay for durable utility before leaning heavily on paid distribution.

Subscriptions should unlock capability rather than guarantee reach. Higher limits, better analytics, advanced project tools, scheduling, team seats, storage, profile customization, business workflows, and collaboration tools are appropriate paid features. Organic first tests should remain available to eligible content regardless of payment status.

Preserve Packs should be designed as storage and permanence products. They can allow users or creators to keep additional work available, increase archival capacity, preserve project history, or extend media retention. The product should avoid implying that Preserve Packs purchase ranking preference.

Advertising should be clearly labeled, reviewed, paced, capped, and measured separately from organic distribution. Sponsored posts, sponsored projects, search placements, and community sponsorships can become strong revenue streams only if users can distinguish paid placements from platform recommendations.

Creator sponsorship tools should help creators and brands find each other through transparent briefs, eligibility rules, pricing support, disclosure requirements, campaign tracking, and brand safety controls. The marketplace should not pressure creators into undisclosed promotion.

Creator payouts should use a disciplined ledger. The platform needs wallet balances, pending balances, available balances, refund reserves, payout thresholds, fraud checks, identity verification where required, statements, payment provider records, tax information where applicable, and support paths for disputes.

Revenue modeling should connect every plan and ad product to cost. Storage, media delivery, processing, moderation, search indexing, support, payment fees, fraud reserves, chargebacks, infrastructure, and internal tooling all affect margin. Growth without cost visibility can make the product look healthier than it is.
